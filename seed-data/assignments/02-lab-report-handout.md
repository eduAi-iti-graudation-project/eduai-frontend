## Scenario

You are a laboratory researcher investigating how temperature affects the rate
of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction. Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up
reactions in living organisms, and their activity is highly sensitive to
temperature.

## Background

Enzymes have an **optimum temperature** at which they work fastest. Below the
optimum, molecular motion is slow and fewer enzyme–substrate collisions occur.
Above the optimum, the enzyme's shape is altered — it becomes **denatured** —
and activity drops sharply. This experiment measures the time for catalase (an
enzyme in potatoes) to break down hydrogen peroxide at different temperatures,
using the height of the oxygen foam produced as a proxy for reaction rate.

## Hypothesis

Write a clear, testable hypothesis before starting, for example:

> "If the temperature increases beyond 40°C, then the rate of the catalase
> reaction will decrease because the enzyme becomes denatured."

## Materials

- Fresh potato (catalase source)
- 3% hydrogen peroxide solution
- Test tubes and test tube rack
- Ice bath (0°C), water baths at 20°C, 40°C, 60°C, and boiling water (100°C)
- Ruler (to measure foam height)
- Stopwatch
- Thermometer

## Procedure

1. Prepare five water baths at 0°C, 20°C, 40°C, 60°C, and 100°C.
2. Cut five equal-sized cubes of potato (1 cm³) — one per temperature.
3. Place one cube in a test tube and add 10 mL of hydrogen peroxide solution.
4. Immediately start the stopwatch; record the **foam height (mm)** after
   60 seconds.
5. Repeat each temperature **three times** and record all trials.
6. Calculate the mean foam height for each temperature.

## Analysis Questions

1. At which temperature was the reaction fastest? What does this tell you about
   the optimum temperature of catalase?
2. What happened to the reaction at 100°C? Explain using the term **denaturation**.
3. Why was it important to use equal-sized potato cubes and the same volume of
   hydrogen peroxide in every trial? (Controlled variables)
4. Identify the independent variable, dependent variable, and at least two
   controlled variables in this experiment.
5. How would you improve the reliability of your results?
