## 2.1 DNA: The Molecule of Heredity

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a double helix made of two strands of nucleotides
held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases.

- **Nucleotide** = phosphate group + deoxyribose sugar + nitrogenous base.
- **Bases**: adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G), cytosine (C).
- **Base-pairing rule**: A pairs with T (two hydrogen bonds); G pairs with C
  (three hydrogen bonds).

A gene is a segment of DNA that codes for a protein or functional RNA. The
complete set of genetic material in an organism is its **genome**.

## 2.2 Chromosomes and Karyotypes

In eukaryotic cells, DNA is wrapped around histone proteins to form
chromosomes. Humans have **46 chromosomes** in 23 pairs:

- 22 pairs of autosomes.
- 1 pair of sex chromosomes (XX in females, XY in males).

A karyotype is a photograph of an organism's chromosomes arranged in pairs.
Karyotypes are used to detect chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome
(trisomy 21).

**Homologous chromosomes** carry the same genes at the same loci, but the
versions (alleles) may differ — one inherited from each parent.

## 2.3 Genes, Alleles, and Genotypes

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Gene | Unit of heredity; a segment of DNA |
| Allele | Alternative version of a gene (e.g., T vs. t) |
| Genotype | The genetic makeup of an individual (e.g., Tt) |
| Phenotype | The observable trait produced by the genotype |
| Homozygous | Two identical alleles (TT or tt) |
| Heterozygous | Two different alleles (Tt) |
| Dominant allele | Expressed even when paired with a recessive allele |
| Recessive allele | Expressed only when two copies are present |

## 2.4 Mendel's Laws

Gregor Mendel's pea-plant experiments (1860s) established the laws of
inheritance.

### Law of Segregation

Each individual carries two alleles for each trait; during gamete formation the
alleles separate so each gamete carries one allele.

### Law of Independent Assortment

Alleles of different genes are inherited independently of one another during
gamete formation (true when the genes are on different chromosomes).

### Monohybrid cross example — pea seed shape

R = round (dominant), r = wrinkled (recessive). Cross two heterozygotes (Rr × Rr):

| Gametes | R | r |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R** | RR | Rr |
| **r** | Rr | rr |

- Genotypic ratio: 1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr
- Phenotypic ratio: 3 round : 1 wrinkled

### Dihybrid cross example — seed shape and color

RrYy × RrYy produces the classic **9:3:3:1** phenotypic ratio
(round-yellow : round-green : wrinkled-yellow : wrinkled-green).

## 2.5 Beyond Simple Dominance

| Pattern | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incomplete dominance | Heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype | Snapdragon flower color: red × white → pink |
| Codominance | Both alleles fully expressed | Human ABO blood type: AB |
| Multiple alleles | More than two alleles exist in a population | ABO system: A, B, O |
| Polygenic inheritance | Many genes contribute to one trait | Human height, skin color |
| Sex-linked inheritance | Gene is located on the X chromosome | Red-green color blindness, hemophilia |

## 2.6 Mutations

A mutation is a permanent change in DNA sequence.

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Point mutation | Single base change (silent, missense, or nonsense) |
| Frameshift mutation | Insertion/deletion that shifts the reading frame |
| Chromosomal mutation | Large-scale change (deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation) |

Mutations can be neutral, harmful, or beneficial, and are the ultimate source of
genetic variation — the raw material for evolution by natural selection.
