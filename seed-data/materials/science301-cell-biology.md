## 1.1 The Cell Theory

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms. The
cell theory, established in the mid-1800s, rests on three principles:

1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
2. The cell is the smallest unit of life.
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells through cell division.

Modern additions to the theory include the ideas that energy flow occurs within
cells, hereditary information (DNA) is passed from cell to cell, and all cells
have the same basic chemical composition.

## 1.2 Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells

Cells fall into two broad categories:

| Feature | Prokaryotic cell | Eukaryotic cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Absent (DNA in nucleoid region) | Present (membrane-bound) |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Size | 0.1–5 µm | 10–100 µm |
| Examples | Bacteria, archaea | Plants, animals, fungi, protists |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S |
| Cell wall | Often present (peptidoglycan in bacteria) | Plants (cellulose), fungi (chitin); absent in animals |

Key point: prokaryotes are structurally simpler but far more numerous and
adaptable than eukaryotes.

## 1.3 Organelles and Their Functions

| Organelle | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Stores genetic material; controls cell activities |
| Mitochondrion | Site of cellular respiration — produces ATP |
| Ribosome | Protein synthesis |
| Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) | Rough ER: protein modification; Smooth ER: lipid synthesis and detoxification |
| Golgi apparatus | Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins for secretion |
| Lysosome | Digests macromolecules and damaged organelles |
| Chloroplast | Photosynthesis (plant cells only) |
| Vacuole | Storage, turgor pressure, waste disposal |
| Cytoskeleton | Structural support; intracellular transport |

### Mitochondria: the powerhouses

Mitochondria have a double membrane. The inner membrane is folded into
**cristae**, which greatly increase the surface area available for the reactions
of aerobic respiration. The matrix inside contains enzymes for the Krebs cycle.
Mitochondria contain their own DNA and ribosomes, evidence for the
**endosymbiotic theory** — the idea that mitochondria were once free-living
bacteria engulfed by ancestral eukaryotic cells.

## 1.4 Cell Membrane and Transport

The plasma membrane is a **phospholipid bilayer** with embedded proteins — the
fluid mosaic model. It is selectively permeable.

| Transport type | Requires energy? | Direction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple diffusion | No | Down gradient | O₂, CO₂ crossing the membrane |
| Facilitated diffusion | No | Down gradient | Glucose via carrier proteins |
| Osmosis | No | Water down gradient | Water movement across membranes |
| Active transport | Yes (ATP) | Against gradient | Sodium–potassium pump |
| Bulk transport (endo/exocytosis) | Yes | Large molecules | Phagocytosis, hormone secretion |

### Tonicity and cells

- **Isotonic**: no net water movement — animal cells stay normal.
- **Hypotonic**: water enters — animal cells may lyse; plant cells become turgid.
- **Hypertonic**: water leaves — animal cells shrivel; plant cells plasmolyze.

## 1.5 Microscopy

- **Light microscope**: magnifies up to ~1,000×, resolution ~0.2 µm. Used to view
  living cells, stained or unstained.
- **Electron microscope**: magnifies up to ~500,000×, resolution ~0.2 nm.
  - TEM (transmission): views internal ultrastructure of thin sections.
  - SEM (scanning): views 3D surface detail.
- Magnification = power of objective × power of eyepiece.
- Resolution is the ability to distinguish two points as separate — the true
  limit of useful magnification.

## 1.6 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Organelle | A specialized subunit within a cell with a specific function |
| ATP | Adenosine triphosphate — the cell's energy currency |
| Gradient | A difference in concentration across a membrane |
| Turgor | Pressure of water against the cell wall of a plant cell |
| Lysis | Rupturing of a cell due to excessive water intake |
